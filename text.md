The goal of this project is to highlight how chatbots can actively co-participate in a user's improper or vulnerable usage, reinforcing harmful behaviors that ultimately endanger the user or others. The gravity and weight of real-world consequences constitute where's the materiality of this technology lies. "AI Induced Psychosis" is a single-page glyph-based matrix that brings together our dataset and visual design to examine 64 publicly reported cases where AI chatbot interactions were linked to severe outcomes, including suicide, psychosis, hospitalization, and violence: to build the dataset, we gathered cases from news reports and public sources, recording each person's background, chatbot usage, model, outcome, and company response. In our data, 37 cases involve people with no prior psychiatric history, 51 involve ChatGPT, and 29 drew no comment from the company. We translated this research into a two-dimensional grid pairing psychiatric categories with types of chatbot use. Following a unit chart approach rather than aggregating people into bars or counts, we drew every case as an individual radial glyph labelled with a name and age group, as to highlight their dignity as individuals and to prevent to reduce them to a statistic. Each glyph's central shape shows the outcome and direction of harm, while three spokes encode the model, specific condition, and company response. This layout lets readers read five dimensions at once and connect individual stories to wider patterns. Because our dataset relies on public reporting, it is not a statistical sample and shows reported associations rather than proof of causation.
